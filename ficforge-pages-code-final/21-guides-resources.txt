  <div id="story-checklist" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">STORY CHECKLIST</span>
    <h2>What to prepare before creating a story</h2>
    <p>
      The Create Story form works best when your main story details are ready before you begin.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Story Title</strong>
        <p>Choose a clear, memorable title that is easy to read and not overloaded with extra symbols.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Synopsis</strong>
        <p>Prepare a short description that explains the premise, main conflict, tone, and reason readers should care.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Story Type</strong>
        <p>Select whether your work is Fanfiction or Original Webnovel so FicForge can organize it properly.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>First Chapter</strong>
        <p>Have your opening chapter ready. The first chapter should give readers a reason to continue.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div id="story-type" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">STORY TYPE</span>
    <h2>Choosing Fanfiction or Original Webnovel</h2>
    <p>
      Story type tells FicForge how your work should be organized.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Fanfiction</strong>
        <p>Choose Fanfiction when your story uses an existing fandom, world, characters, setting, or source material. Add the fandom name clearly so readers can find it.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Original Webnovel</strong>
        <p>Choose Original Webnovel when your story is built from your own original characters, setting, world, and core concept.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div id="synopsis" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">SYNOPSIS GUIDE</span>
    <h2>Writing a stronger synopsis</h2>
    <p>
      Your synopsis should tell readers what kind of journey they are entering without revealing every major twist.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Start with the premise</strong>
        <p>Explain the main idea of the story in plain language. Readers should understand the setup quickly.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Show the conflict</strong>
        <p>Let readers know what pressure, danger, romance, mystery, war, rivalry, or personal struggle drives the story.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Make the tone clear</strong>
        <p>Signal whether the story is dark, comedic, romantic, political, action-heavy, tragic, slow-burn, or adventurous.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Avoid empty hype</strong>
        <p>Instead of saying your story is “epic,” describe what actually makes it interesting.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div id="metadata" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">DISCOVERY</span>
    <h2>Genres, fandoms, and tags</h2>
    <p>
      Accurate metadata helps the right readers find your story.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Genres</strong>
        <p>Use broad categories such as Fantasy, Science Fiction, Romance, Horror, Mystery, Action, Adventure, Historical, LitRPG, Drama, War, or Political Intrigue.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Fandoms</strong>
        <p>For fanfiction, name the main fandom clearly. For crossovers, include the major fandoms without overcrowding the field.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Tags</strong>
        <p>Use tags for tropes, tone, relationships, setting, pacing, and major elements like Reincarnation, Slow Burn, Kingdom Building, or Time Travel.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Accuracy Matters</strong>
        <p>Do not use popular tags that do not apply. Misleading tags damage reader trust.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div id="ratings" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">RATINGS & WARNINGS</span>
    <h2>Help readers choose safely</h2>
    <p>
      Ratings and warnings help readers decide what they are comfortable reading.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Pick the honest rating</strong>
        <p>Choose the rating based on the strongest content in the story, not the average chapter.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Use warnings for major content</strong>
        <p>Warn for major themes such as graphic violence, sexual content, abuse themes, self-harm themes, major character death, or extreme horror.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Update when needed</strong>
        <p>When your story grows darker or more intense, update the rating and warnings.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Use the Content Policy</strong>
        <p>For detailed rules about Mature, Explicit, prohibited content, and safety boundaries, check the Content Policy page.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div id="covers" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">COVER IMAGES</span>
    <h2>Make your story cover readable</h2>
    <p>
      A cover should be clean, readable, and suited to the story’s tone.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Readable Title</strong>
        <p>Make sure the title can still be read when the cover appears smaller on mobile or in story lists.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Match the Genre</strong>
        <p>A horror story, romance, war epic, and sci-fi adventure should not all feel visually identical.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Use Permitted Images</strong>
        <p>Use your own art, licensed assets, public-domain materials, or images you have permission to use.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Simple Is Fine</strong>
        <p>A clean text-based cover is better than a crowded image that looks messy or unreadable.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div id="formatting" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">CHAPTER FORMATTING</span>
    <h2>Make chapters comfortable to read</h2>
    <p>
      Clean formatting keeps readers immersed in the story.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Break paragraphs clearly</strong>
        <p>Avoid giant walls of text. Break paragraphs naturally around dialogue, action, description, and emotional beats.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Preview after pasting</strong>
        <p>When copying from Google Docs or Microsoft Word, preview the chapter to make sure spacing and line breaks still look right.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Use author notes carefully</strong>
        <p>Keep notes helpful and brief. Avoid interrupting the middle of the chapter with unrelated announcements.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Think mobile first</strong>
        <p>Many readers will use phones. Shorter paragraphs and clean spacing make long chapters easier to read.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div id="publishing" class="ff-guide-panel">
    <span class="label">PUBLISHING HABITS</span>
    <h2>Build reader trust over time</h2>
    <p>
      Strong creators build trust through consistency, clean presentation, and honest communication.
    </p>

    <div class="ff-guide-list">
      <div>
        <strong>Set realistic update goals</strong>
        <p>A steady weekly or biweekly schedule is often better than promising daily chapters and burning out.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Keep story details updated</strong>
        <p>Update your synopsis, tags, rating, warnings, and status when the story changes direction.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Respect reader expectations</strong>
        <p>Readers should not feel tricked by misleading tags, hidden warnings, or false update promises.</p>
      </div>

      <div>
        <strong>Use support pages</strong>
        <p>For deeper rules about copyright, content limits, creator rights, Sparks, payouts, or moderation, use the dedicated Support pages.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
